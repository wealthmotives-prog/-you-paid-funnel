You Paid Incident Response Runbook

Internal operating runbook. Prelaunch. Last updated October 22, 2026.

Priority: protect people and data first, preserve evidence second, restore safely third. Do not destroy logs or overwrite evidence during containment.

1. Detect & classify

  1. Open a security incident record immediately.
  2. Classify severity based on data exposure, unauthorized access, payment impact, service availability, and consumer harm.
  3. Record detection time, affected systems, known indicators, and reporter.

2. Escalate

  1. Incident Commander owns coordination and timeline.
  2. Technical Lead owns containment, forensic preservation, and recovery.
  3. Legal & Privacy evaluates notification, contractual, regulatory, and evidence-preservation obligations.

3. Contain

  1. Revoke or rotate compromised credentials where supported.
  2. Disable affected integration, endpoint, account, or deployment if continued operation increases risk.
  3. Preserve relevant Supabase, Vercel, GitHub, Stripe, authentication, and application logs.

4. Investigate

  1. Build a timestamped event chronology.
  2. Identify affected users, records, systems, credentials, vendors, and data categories.
  3. Separate confirmed facts from hypotheses.

5. Recover

  1. Remediate root cause before restoring affected functionality.
  2. Verify access controls, data integrity, payment integrity, and monitoring.
  3. Use staged restoration when blast radius is uncertain.

6. Notify when required

Legal & Privacy determines whether member, partner, insurer, law-enforcement, contractual, or regulatory notification is required and documents the analysis. Do not promise a universal notification timeline without jurisdiction-specific review.

7. Close & learn

  1. Document root cause and corrective actions.
  2. Record lessons learned and unresolved risks.
  3. Update controls, runbooks, vendor reviews, and training.
  4. Conduct a tabletop exercise before verification-data scale and after material architecture changes.
