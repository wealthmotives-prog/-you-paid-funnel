Verification Standard v1.1

Verify the event. Preserve the date. Show the source.

You Paid is being designed to verify both historical and ongoing work activity. A legitimate historical event does not become less true because it is verified later. The record should preserve when the work happened, when You Paid verified it, and what evidence supported the decision.

1. The two-date rule

Underlying event date ≠ verification date.

Underlying event date

The date or period when the employment, clock-in, workday, payroll cycle, assignment, or other supported activity actually occurred.

Verification date

The date You Paid reviewed or received sufficient evidence to classify that historical or current fact as verified.

A record may therefore truthfully show a 2024 work event verified in 2026. That is historical verification, not backdating a You Paid account or pretending You Paid observed the event in 2024.

2. Scope

What You Paid may verify.

Relationship

Whether an employment or qualifying work relationship existed during a defined period.

Presence

Supported clock-ins, workdays, shifts, assignments, or comparable work activity.

Continuity

Recurring supported activity across defined weeks, months, or other cycles.

Tenure

How long a qualifying work relationship existed when commencement and continuation can be supported.

Payroll continuity

Recurring payroll evidence that supports ongoing work or compensation continuity without necessarily proving every shift.

Neutral interruptions

Documented periods such as leave, source outages, or other circumstances where no negative inference should automatically be drawn.

3. Evidence hierarchy

Not all evidence is equal.

LevelIllustrative evidenceIntended treatment
L1Member assertion without corroborationUseful for intake, but not sufficient by itself for verified status.
L2Member-provided pay statements, schedules, dated work records, tax/payroll documentsMay support historical verification after identity, authenticity, date, and relevance review.
L3Employer, payroll, staffing, recognized timekeeping, or other external corroborationStronger evidence because it comes from an authoritative or independently controlled source.
L4Continuous source-linked verification from an approved integrationStrongest recurring evidence when source identity, account linkage, timestamps, and integrity are maintained.

These levels are You Paid verification categories, not credit scores, ratings, or promises of third-party acceptance.

4. Minimum record attributes

A verified fact should be reconstructable.

Member

Who the record belongs to and how identity was matched.

Source entity

Employer, payroll provider, timekeeping system, staffing/gig platform, bank-derived payroll source, or other evidence origin.

Underlying date

When the supported event or period actually occurred.

Verification date

When sufficient evidence was accepted by You Paid.

Evidence level

The strength category applied to the evidence supporting the fact.

Audit trail

Material reviews, corrections, disputes, source changes, and status transitions.

5. Historical records

Historical verification can reach behind enrollment.

A member may submit or authorize access to evidence covering periods before the person discovered or joined You Paid. If the evidence reliably supports those historical facts, the work event may be recorded with the actual underlying date and a later You Paid verification date. The member's You Paid membership date is never rewritten to match the historical event.

6. Provider authorization

Authoritative sources make the network stronger.

Where feasible, You Paid intends to obtain data through defined relationships with employers, payroll companies, timekeeping systems, staffing firms, gig platforms, or other qualifying providers. The system is designed to track who supplied the data, the scope of authorization, and whether the authorization remains active.

7. Record states

Uncertainty should remain visible.

Verified

Evidence meets the applicable standard for the fact being recorded.

Pending

Evidence exists but review is incomplete.

Neutral

No negative inference should automatically be drawn.

Unavailable

Insufficient reliable evidence exists to decide.

Disputed

A material accuracy or meaning question requires review.

Rejected

The available evidence cannot support the claimed fact.

8. Corrections and disputes

A reporting-ready record needs an investigation trail.

Members should be able to identify a disputed record, explain the issue, submit supporting evidence, and receive a documented outcome. The system is now structured to distinguish received, investigating, source-contacted, verified-accurate, modified, deleted, and closed dispute states.

9. Reporting transmissions

Verification and furnishing are different operations.

A fact can be verified before any third-party reporting relationship exists. You Paid is being structured to separately track destinations, transmission type, reporting period, format, send status, acknowledgement, rejection, correction, and deletion. No destination should be shown as active unless a real relationship and accepted transmission path exist.

10. Payment separation

Money does not buy truth.

Founder tier, subscription level, referral activity, or payment status should never change the evidentiary standard applied to work-history information.

11. External use

Reporting-ready does not mean reporting-live.

You Paid is deliberately building data structures that can support future nationwide bureau furnishing, specialty consumer reporting, reporting-intermediary arrangements, or direct permissioned data products. None of those routes should be represented as active until the specific destination, agreement, legal requirements, testing, and operational controls are actually in place.
