Verification Standard v1.0

Evidence determines verification.

This public standard describes how You Paid intends to decide whether work-history information is supported, uncertain, disputed, neutral, or rejected. It is a prelaunch operating standard and may evolve as verification sources and regulatory requirements mature.

See the product architectureTrust & Transparency
1. Scope

What can be verified.

Employment relationship

Whether an employment or qualifying work relationship existed during a defined period.

Work presence

Evidence that a member showed up, clocked in, completed a qualifying workday, or otherwise demonstrated supported work activity.

Continuity

Supported evidence of recurring work activity across weeks or months.

Tenure

The length of an employment or work relationship when commencement and continuity can be supported.

2. Source hierarchy

Not every source carries the same evidentiary weight.

LevelIllustrative sourceIntended treatment
L1Member self-attestation without corroborating evidenceMay support intake or review, but should not independently create a verified event.
L2Member-provided documentary evidence, such as a pay statement or dated work recordMay support verification after authenticity, date, identity, and relevance checks.
L3Employer, payroll, or recognized timekeeping corroborationStronger evidence because the source is external to the member and tied to work activity.
L4Continuous or recurring source-linked verification from a trusted integrationStrongest recurring evidence when source integrity and account linkage are maintained.

These levels are internal/public design categories, not credit-rating grades and not promises of external acceptance.

3. Required attributes

A verified fact should be traceable.

Subject

Which member the evidence belongs to.

Period

The date, workday, pay period, or month actually supported.

Source

Where the evidence came from and how it was obtained.

Event type

Clock-in, workday, payroll, employment status, document, or another defined type.

Decision state

Pending, verified, disputed, rejected, neutral, or another specifically defined state.

Audit history

When the record was created, reviewed, changed, or corrected.

4. Record states

Uncertainty should not be hidden.

Verified

Available evidence meets the applicable standard for the fact being recorded.

Pending

Evidence has been submitted or identified but review is not complete.

Neutral

No negative inference should be drawn. Examples may include approved leave, a period with no applicable obligation, or another documented neutral circumstance.

Verification unavailable

The system does not currently have enough reliable evidence to decide.

Disputed

The member or another relevant party challenges the accuracy or meaning of the record and review is required.

Rejected

The evidence is insufficient, contradictory, unverifiable, or otherwise cannot support the claimed fact.

5. Neutrality and interruptions

Job loss is not automatically failure.

Employment termination, protected leave, illness, scheduling changes, data-source outages, or other interruptions should not automatically be converted into derogatory performance. You Paid intends to distinguish absence of evidence, absence of an applicable work obligation, and actual failure to satisfy a defined obligation.

6. Historical tenure

Historical facts are not backdated You Paid history.

If reliable evidence shows that a member began working for an employer before joining You Paid, the employment start date may reflect the supported historical fact. The member's You Paid relationship and You Paid verification history still begin when those relationships actually begin.

7. Corrections and disputes

A durable record needs a correction path.

Members should be able to identify a record they believe is inaccurate, explain the issue, submit supporting information, and receive a review outcome. A disputed record should not silently remain represented as unquestioned verified fact while a material accuracy issue is under review.

8. Payment separation

Money does not buy a stronger fact.

Founder tier, subscription level, referral activity, or any other payment status should not change the evidentiary standard applied to a member's work-history record.

9. External use

Verification is not the same as external acceptance.

A You Paid record may eventually be made available for member-authorized use by third parties. This standard does not mean any credit bureau, lender, landlord, employer, insurer, scoring model, or other third party currently accepts or relies on the record.
